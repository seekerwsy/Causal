\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Evaluation Overview}

The evaluation examines two complementary aspects of security guidance: the
evidence supporting a proposed modification and the perceived utility of its
explanation. RQ1 asks whether the analysis identifies actionable hypotheses
that yield meaningful effect evidence under a fixed budget. RQ2 examines the
structural information used to identify them. RQ3 evaluates the prompt policies
underlying the guidance, and RQ4 examines experts' assessments of the complete
explanation products. The computational and human studies address different
claims; neither substitutes for the other.

The computational study uses disjoint development,
discovery, and held-out confirmation task units. All included selectors must
share the same prospectively frozen task manifests, generator and request-randomness policy, candidate
budgets, common hypothesis bridge, randomized confirmation, and independent
measurement rules. Candidate identities, selector slots, prompt policies, and
inference rules must be fixed before confirmation outcomes. Each unique selected
model-bound effect is confirmed once and its result is reused by every
referencing selector slot.

\paragraph{Tasks and experimental settings.}
Natural programming tasks must specify an interpretable operation, the relevant
security boundary, and the non-target behavior to preserve. Incomplete functional
specifications limit functionality and joint-success claims without automatically
excluding an independently measurable security comparison. Source lineage and
task-unit deduplication govern the role split. The final source inventory,
admitted language and task families, model configurations, task counts,
generation-request counts, and Atomic/Pair budgets are to be fixed jointly with
Oracle qualification and the comparator envelope before formal execution.

\subsection{RQ1: Overall Effectiveness}

\textbf{RQ1. How effectively does \model identify actionable prompt-side security hypotheses compared with state-of-the-art methods?}

RQ1 evaluates which requirement-level questions receive useful follow-up
evidence. Selection is assessed by the held-out effects obtained for the
original budget of hypotheses, rather than by observational scores alone.
The planned comparison covers representative state-of-the-art methods for
prompt-side security analysis, alongside matched ablations and reference
rankings. Association, regularized prediction, LLM-based explanation,
causality-aided analysis, and natural-language perturbation define candidate
comparator families, not already qualified implementations. Each included
method must produce a fixed number of context--requirement--operation slots
through a prospectively specified bridge. The exact methods, bridge validation,
and shared evaluation budget require a joint freeze; Expert and seeded Random
rankings are included only when that budget covers them. Comparisons over a
shared candidate universe assess selection, whereas methods operating in native
output spaces require separately identified end-to-end comparisons that also
include their mapping failures.

The method-level endpoint is meaningful Yield@$K$: the proportion of the
original $K$ slots with positive- or negative-meaningful held-out effects.
Empty slots, failed gates, protocolization failures, and non-evaluable findings
remain in the denominator. The complete candidate-to-confirmation funnel
reports their composition, candidate overlap, and positive versus negative
findings. This endpoint measures discovery of effect-bearing hypotheses, not
explanation quality or the fraction of successful hardening policies. Yield differences describe the
frozen benchmark; they do not establish expected superiority over arbitrary
discovery populations.

\subsection{RQ2: Component Contribution}

\textbf{RQ2. Which components of \model contribute to its effectiveness in identifying actionable security hypotheses?}

RQ2 tests whether the structural admission criteria contribute to that
selection objective. Two controlled comparisons isolate the information used in
prioritization. Atomic Full versus RD-only holds the candidate universe,
task rows, support rules, folds, first-order RD scores, tie-breaking, and $K_A$
fixed; only Full applies the qualified FCI adjacency gate. Pair Full versus
No-Relation holds compatible-pair rows, four-cell support, folds, second-order
RD scores, tie-breaking, and $K_I$ fixed; only Full applies the Prompt-TSG
relation gate. Neither Pair selector requires Atomic selection or support.
Both comparisons share the bridge and downstream confirmation results.

We report Full-minus-ablation meaningful Yield@$K$, selected-candidate overlap,
and the empty-slot and failure composition. These tests isolate the two
structural gates; they do not independently establish the contribution of every
representation or measurement component. In particular, these Gate ablations
do not test whether a graph-grounded explanation is easier to understand.
That claim would require a separately designed explanation comparison.

\subsection{RQ3: Prompt Hardening Effectiveness}

\textbf{RQ3. Can security explanations produced by \model guide prompt hardening that reduces insecure code generation?}

RQ3 evaluates the effects of the prompt modifications described in the
explanations. It asks which changes support a hardening conclusion under
Section~\ref{sec:method-guidance}, rather than treating every selected or
effect-bearing hypothesis as beneficial. This is a computational evaluation
of the underlying policies, not a test of whether a person can read an
explanation and successfully apply its advice.
For Atomic policies, the primary contrast is target versus
operation-matched no-op on oracle-evaluable secure-code yield. Placebo/sham and
generic-security controls assess specificity under separately frozen secondary
inference rules. ADD and REMOVE remain distinct policies. Pair policies use
$A_{00}$, $A_{10}$, $A_{01}$, and $A_{11}$ to estimate the two-factor response
surface and its primary two-sided RD interaction; an interaction alone does not
show that the joint-target arm improves security.

Effects are model-specific, realization-weighted assigned-arm ITT estimates
with task-unit uncertainty and the separate Atomic and Pair simultaneous
families in Section~\ref{sec:method}. Every committed assignment remains in its
assigned arm regardless of fidelity, compliance, generation success, or
non-target drift. We distinguish positive-meaningful, negative-meaningful, practically null,
inconclusive, and non-evaluable evidence. Validity, evaluability, insecure and
unknown labels, functionality, and joint success are reported separately,
together with coverage sensitivity bounds. Thus, observed secure-yield gains
are not automatically reported as reductions in the underlying insecure-code
rate, and beneficial hardening claims require the corresponding security and
task-preservation evidence.

\subsection{RQ4: Explanation Quality}

\textbf{RQ4. How do security experts evaluate the quality of explanations produced by \model and competing methods?}

RQ4 addresses whether experts find the resulting explanations clear, credible,
and useful for deciding how to modify a prompt. The comparison unit is each
method's complete explanation product, including the requirement it identifies,
the evidence it provides, and the guidance or limits it communicates. A common
presentation identifies context, editable requirement, comparison, evidence,
uncertainty, and scope without inventing evidence unavailable from a method.
The study therefore evaluates overall perceived utility, not alternative
wordings of identical evidence. Differences in ratings cannot be attributed
solely to Prompt TSG or to the prose format. Methods included as RQ1 selectors
also need a specified explanation-construction procedure to enter this comparison.

The planned human study will compare anonymized products under a balanced
case--method assignment. Candidate rating dimensions include clarity,
evidence sufficiency, credibility, actionability, and usefulness. Case sampling
must not be restricted to favorable examples. The plan must specify how the
available effect statuses in Section~\ref{sec:method-causal} are represented,
distinguishing Atomic policy effects from Pair interactions.
Participant criteria and counts, comparator materials, case sampling,
presentation order, scales, the primary endpoint, ethics determination, and an
analysis accounting for repeated ratings by expert and case must be fixed before
recruitment or response inspection and recorded in the preregistration.
The RQ1 Expert selector is distinct from
these study participants and ratings.

RQ4 evaluates perceived explanation quality and utility. Rated credibility does
not establish causal correctness, and rated clarity or actionability does not
establish objective comprehension, successful adoption, or repair accuracy.
These ratings cannot revise the computational evidence used in RQ1--RQ3.
They provide a separate evaluation of whether the evidence presentation is
useful to its intended expert audience.
