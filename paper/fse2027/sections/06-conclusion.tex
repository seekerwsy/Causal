\section{Conclusion}

\model turns the broad question of whether prompt wording matters into a
requirement-level empirical question. A typed Prompt TSG defines the applicable
context and valid ADD or REMOVE operation; observational discovery prioritizes
and freezes the hypotheses worth a fixed evaluation budget; and held-out
blocked randomization estimates the assigned prompt-policy effects on
independently measured security and functionality. Under this design, a
requirement matters at the effect level only when its adjusted randomized
interval excludes zero in the frozen scope, becomes beneficial only in the
security-improving direction, and becomes practically actionable only with
supported specificity and preserved functionality. The requirement evidence
record keeps these conclusions, their uncertainty, and their scope together,
providing a direct and reviewable route from natural-language prompt structure
to usable security evidence.
